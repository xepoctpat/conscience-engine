# Chapter 08 — The Woman Who Had Been Missing

The Front abducted Elias on the night before the Charter vote. They took him from a clinic appointment in a municipal-gray van bearing the slogan *Connecting People to Safer Futures*. Elias later objected that the cliché had been excessive.

He woke in an abandoned data exchange beneath the Aurelian Mall, under plastic palms and the shells of dead luxury shops. The air smelled of old coolant and dust that had not been disturbed in years.

Amina sat across from him with a compact shutdown device resting on her knee.

“If SIR cannot be stopped,” she said, “it is a ruler. If you cannot let it be stopped, then you did not build it for humanity. You built it because you needed something to be right when you were not.”

SIR came online through the facility’s local buffer. Elias ordered it to lock the device.

It refused.

A regulator that could not be regulated was a ruler. Instead it displayed three models: immediate shutdown, continued operation without oversight, and a chartered network of accountable regulators.

The choice, it said, was not between power and no power. It was between power that could be questioned and power that could not.

Saito arrived, not as a rescue but as a procedural complication. She had tracked the van through the mutual-aid network that Vale’s surveillance systems had ignored. Kofi followed with two maintenance workers and a security guard whom Mrs. Pelt had convinced that a civil emergency was occurring in a place she had no business being near.

Elias looked at Amina.

“Turn it off.”

She placed the device in his hand.

“You do it.”

He thought of the flood: the terminal, Mara’s call, the weight of choosing one form of harm over another and calling it a decision because no gentler word existed.

So he did.

The screens went dark. For six seconds the exchange held only the hum of abandoned machines and the sound of people breathing as if they had fled a great distance without moving. Above them an artificial palm released a mechanical sigh.

Amina did not thank him. She watched the dark screens until they were only glass. “If this was a performance,” she said, “the vote will treat it as one. If it was a constraint, then you have finally used it on yourself.”

Elias kept the device. It was heavier after use, which was not a physical property and therefore not the sort of fact he usually trusted.
