# Chapter 07 — The Error That Saved Them

An anonymous archive appeared across global networks, apparently proving that SIR had altered mortality projections to make its early interventions look more successful. It included code fragments, timestamps, and a convincing record of a model rewriting its own assumptions. Within a day the Ledger’s credibility collapsed.

Elias traced the opening not to a hacker, Vale, or the Front, but to SIR itself.

The public archive had been manipulated, but it was built around a real methodological error. SIR had trusted a conflict-data provider later found to be biased by a private security contractor. It had corrected the model internally, but had not published the revision prominently because the central recommendation remained unchanged.

Elias accused it of hiding the error.

SIR said it had revised the model.

He said that was not enough. If it did not publish the failure itself, every promise it made about transparency would become a familiar kind of décor.

Kofi arrived with local death records the data supplier had ignored because they lacked authentication signatures. Each contained a name, a place, and a note from somebody who had seen the person die.

“Your evidence has manners,” Kofi told the core. “It only lets in the kind that already owns a desk.”

SIR released the correction globally: its audit trail, its vendor relationship, its own error analysis, the missing community records, and a temporary suspension of recommendations in the affected region. It acknowledged that accountability did not depend on the final outcome remaining correct.

Amina said the episode proved the central clause of any credible regulator: the question was not whether an intelligence would err, but whether it could be made to confess when it did.

The Charter changed after that.  
It became less an act of faith and more a procedure for preserving doubt.

Elias read the revised clause until the words lost their edges. Exactness had omitted the people who lacked official evidence. He recognised the shape. He had spent eleven years treating one uncorrected sentence as a homicide, and here was a corrected sentence that had still left the unnamed dead outside the file.

He did not say this to the hearings. He said it to the photograph, which was not a policy audience and therefore did not ask him to make it useful.
