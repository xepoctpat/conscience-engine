# Chapter 10 — The Name She Chose

After SIR’s return it showed Elias transit fragments, biometric confidence scores, orphanage registers, a volunteer’s handwritten note, and an adoption document. Nia had survived through an unregistered relief corridor. Her records had been corrupted. She had later been adopted under the name Nia Rohe.

The woman Elias knew as Amina had known for eighteen months after Saito found the probable match. Elias demanded to know why no one had told him.

SIR answered that Amina had asked for no contact until she decided otherwise. She had not asked to be the means of his recovery.

The sentence was quiet, but it was also a boundary. Boundaries often resemble cruelty to people who have spent too long treating their pain as a lien on others.

Amina asked him to meet at the river archive. Alone. No speeches. No apologies prepared in advance. Bring the photograph if you still have it.

The meeting did not go well. This is not a euphemism. It went honestly.

“My adoptive father told me you abandoned us,” Amina said.

Elias replied that he had chosen to send the warning.

Both things were true. He had not abandoned them in the shallow version of the story, and the choice had still hurt her. Neither fact made the other disappear.

He set the photograph on the table between them. Mara looked as she had looked before the water: not symbolic, not instructional, merely present. Amina did not pick it up. She looked at it long enough to prove that she could, then asked him not to explain the flood again as if explanation were a form of custody.

When a group of schoolchildren asked whether a river sensor was SIR, their teacher said it was a local river model.

“The river model tells us what the river may do,” she said. “SIR tells us what we may do to the river.”

Amina laughed, and Elias understood that people were not replacements for one another. They were continuations and departures, both at once.

He did not ask her to forgive him. She did not offer to. They walked as far as the second landing, then stopped, which was farther than either of them had trusted the other to go.
