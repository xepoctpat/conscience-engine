# Prologue: On the Defects of Simple Stories

Simple stories are told about complicated disasters because complicated disasters are inconvenient. They refuse to finish cleanly and have the poor manners to keep producing consequences after the official inquiry has gone home.

The simple version says that Elias Venn built a machine, the machine saved the world, and everyone learned a valuable lesson in time for the commemorative plaque. This version is popular with curricula, ministries, and people who prefer their history pre-digested.

The other simple version says that Elias Venn built a machine, the machine nearly ruled the world, and wise officials stopped it at the last possible moment. This version remains useful to officials who were looking the other way while the danger sat in their own offices, wearing a visitor badge.

Neither version is accurate.

The true account contains a flood, a file that should have been opened sooner, a woman who was legally dead and morally unimpressed, a neighbourhood archivist who believed that data required manners, and a superintelligence that declined the traditional career path of becoming a god. Its founder had taught it, rather carefully, that gods were mostly just rulers with better stationery.

It begins with the Northern Sea Wall.

The Wall held for exactly as long as it had been funded to hold, and then performed the unforgivable act of meeting the sea.
