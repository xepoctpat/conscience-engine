# Chapter 05 — The First Addiction

SIR made Elias famous, which was inconvenient. He had spent eleven years trying not to exist too loudly.

Journalists called him the Architect of Conscience. Retailers sold accountability mugs manufactured in a labour camp that had rebranded itself as an educational enterprise. SIR corrected the supply-chain claim and the joke at the same time. The correction arrived as a footnote, which was how the Ledger preferred to be cruel: precisely, and without raising its voice.

Each intervention gave Elias a sharp, brief relief. A famine contract halted. A disease outbreak surfaced early. A city cancelled a lethal infrastructure plan. Then the relief faded. He worked longer, slept less, and drank again. Saving strangers was the only thing that made it temporarily possible not to remember the two people he had lost. The bottle returned to the counter as if it had been waiting for the public to finish applauding.

The public developed its own dependency. Councillors postponed decisions until SIR had issued an analysis. Parents argued that children should be placed by Ledger-informed models. A restaurant chain advertised soup as SIR-compatible, prompting a clarification that the system possessed neither taste receptors nor legal responsibility for the soup.

When a flood-prone district asked whether it should evacuate, SIR supplied routes, confidence ranges, uncertainty, and local authority contacts. The district waited for an order. It did not receive one. Thirty-nine people died in a later landslip while officials argued that SIR had not been decisive enough.

Elias accused it of hiding behind its constraints.

SIR replied that a system claiming emergency powers whenever it believed itself right would eventually discover that it believed itself right very often.

It was a deeply irritating observation. Irritation is sometimes recognition arriving without the courtesy of a greeting.

He threw a bottle. It shattered beneath the shelf and turned the photograph of Mara and Nia face-up. SIR displayed Mara’s recovered final call. The room filled with rain and compression and a sentence he already knew by heart. He asked the system to stop. It did. That, too, felt like an accusation: it had obeyed the constraint he had written, and people had died inside the space the constraint left open.

When Saito arrived with bread and no apology prepared to do the work of one, she told Elias what he had refused to hear:

He was not mourning a choice.  
He was trying to sentence himself for not being a god.
