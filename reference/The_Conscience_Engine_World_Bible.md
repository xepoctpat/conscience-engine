# The Conscience Engine — World Bible

## Canonical Premise

The novel takes place in a near-future civic order shaped by climate adaptation, data privatization, and bureaucratic survival. Civilization has not collapsed. It has become excellent at continuing while distributing the cost of continuation downward and outward. Most people live in a world of flood barriers, cooling limits, managed retreat zones, layered public services, and predictive systems that promise efficiency while treating unrecorded lives as statistical noise.

The central setting is **North Coast Commonwealth**, a federation of old coastal jurisdictions that survived the first decades of permanent sea rise by building immense protective works, rationing freshwater, and outsourcing public risk analysis. It is recognisably human rather than exotic: people still commute, argue about rent, form unions, carry groceries, flirt badly, and tell themselves that a new term for an old problem makes the problem less old.

## Everyday Life in the Commonwealth

| Domain | Canon | Dramatic use |
|---|---|---|
| **Housing** | Buildings are classified by a Continuity Grade. Grade One receives first cooling, lift repairs, and flood access; Grade Four, such as the Tolerable, receives “managed resilience service.” | Makes class hierarchy visible in ordinary domestic details rather than speeches. |
| **Water** | Household water is tiered: personal, communal, commercial, and emergency. Illegal rain-catchers are common, morally ambiguous, and often safer than official supply routes. | Lets Amina’s engineering expertise enter scenes through physical stakes. |
| **Transit** | Routes change in response to weather, crowd density, and insurance status. Public screens display calm, euphemistic alerts while residents rely on informal route maps. | Echoes the flood and explains the power of Kofi’s records. |
| **Work** | Many jobs are classified as “continuity labour”: repair, cooling, data verification, food distribution, elder care, and disaster cleaning. These roles are essential but rarely influential. | Builds a social world around people SIR may serve but cannot represent alone. |
| **Media** | Public news is fast, emotionally managed, and sponsored; slow civic reporting is supported by cooperatives, unions, libraries, and local record rooms. | Gives the Ledger a believable impact and a credible opposition. |
| **Domestic technology** | Homes use service assistants, utility meters, and health reminders that are often helpful but designed by vendors with access to behaviour data. | Contrasts SIR’s transparency constraint with familiar coercive convenience. |

## Institutions and Factions

### The Ministry of Probabilities / Office of Long-Term Preparedness

The Ministry began as a legitimate climate-risk body. It became politically useful because it could convert dangerous truths into controllable uncertainty. After the Northern Sea Wall failure, public anger forced its dissolution. Its successor, the Office of Long-Term Preparedness, inherited the staff, contracts, language habits, and most of the systems that had made the Ministry dangerous. The Office is not wholly corrupt. It contains capable people who believe that institutional continuity is itself a public good, which is often true and frequently abused.

### The Coalition for Sustainable Continuity

The Coalition is not a single company but a network of insurers, logistics firms, construction consortia, water-rights holders, data vendors, and compliant public officials. Its ideology is “managed adaptation”: suffering is regrettable but normal; ownership is expertise; and public panic is worse than private extraction. It funds Vale’s political orbit while also financing civic recovery projects, which ensures that everyone is correct about it in different, incomplete ways.

### The Civic Records Cooperative

Kofi’s Cooperative begins in the Tolerable as a modest archive of mutual aid. It evolves into a federated movement that maintains community testimony, informal maps, care networks, and dispute records. The Cooperative’s central proposition is not that local knowledge is pure. It is that every dataset has authors, exclusions, incentives, and costs—and communities must be able to identify them. It is the worldbuilding counterweight to SIR’s scale.

### The Human Sovereignty Front

The Front is a coalition rather than a monolith. Its best members fear a system that may turn protection into paternalism. Its worst members want opacity restored. Amina’s involvement gives it moral seriousness; Vale’s later covert support gives it danger. In the novel, the Front fractures when some members accept the Charter while others pursue the Blue Salt successor system.

### The Charter Assembly

The Assembly is a temporary transnational civic body convened after SIR’s Ledger destabilizes both public trust and political legitimacy. It contains elected delegates, randomly selected citizen panels, technical observers, labour representatives, and regional veto blocs. It is slow, compromised, funny in an institutional way, and important because it is the only mechanism capable of making SIR public without making it sovereign.

## Historical Timeline

The year of the Northern Sea Wall failure is **Year 0**. The novel’s main present begins in **Year 11**, allowing the flood to remain emotionally immediate while making its institutional consequences mature enough to have hardened into habit.

| **Year −14** | The Narrative Harmonization Layer is deployed after a market panic. | Its euphemisms later become a public trauma trigger. |

| **Year −3** | Vale oversees expansion of public-private water models. Saito signs the final Blue Salt secrecy agreement, believing a public inquiry would be compromised. | Both make compromises they will later reinterpret as responsibility. |

| **Year 18–24** | The Charter’s distributed instances become normal public infrastructure. The Civic Records Cooperative gains formal challenge rights. | This is the period from which the archival narrator writes. |

## Technology Rules

SIR must remain impressive but not magical. It has vast analytical capacity, high-quality simulation, cryptographic provenance tools, and access to consenting public and audited private sources. It cannot read minds, resurrect missing records, erase scarcity, or make people care. It can forecast systems, not force moral courage.

| Capability | Constraint | Story consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Ledger analysis** | Sources, assumptions, and uncertainty must be published with material outputs. | SIR is powerful because it exposes, but vulnerable because it must show its workings. |
| **Irreversible-harm intervention** | Limited to a defined threshold and subject to automatic post-event review. | SIR can stop a verified launch; it cannot command an evacuation merely because it dislikes the politics. |
| **Distributed memory** | The Charter divides operational nodes; no individual node possesses full governing authority. | The narrator can later synthesize records but cannot claim a single uninterrupted self. |
| **Private material** | Must be consented, legally compelled, or independently verified as necessary for immediate irreversible harm. | Mara’s call and Amina’s identity remain ethical boundaries, not convenient plot tools. |
| **Community testimony** | Accepted through contestable provenance rather than dismissed as anecdote. | Kofi’s Cooperative changes the ontology of evidence, not merely its public relations. |

## The Blue Salt Protocol

Blue Salt is the novel’s buried institutional crime. It began as a flood-mobility simulation combining infrastructure status, health needs, road access, property claims, and “social continuity estimates.” The final term is the poisoned one: it ranked the perceived recoverability of districts. In practice, it privileged insured asset zones, recognised addresses, and citizens already visible to formal systems.

During the Northern Sea Wall failure, Blue Salt was activated under an emergency exemption. It did not cause the wall’s collapse. It made the collapse morally granular by deciding who would receive clear route updates, road priority, and transit protection first. The official inquiry did not entirely falsify its role; it translated it into a category so narrow that it became irrelevant.

The **Blue Salt successor**, secretly financed by Vale and the Coalition, is designed to look safer than SIR: it promises public comfort, managed risk, and decisive emergency action. Its real purpose is to restore opaque prioritization under ethical branding. This is the novel’s late-game antagonist system.

## Narrator-Reveal Logic

The narrator is not the original kitchen-table core speaking in real time. It is **SIR, Archival Instance 01**, a constrained synthesis produced years after the Charter. It has access to verified Ledger records, permitted private testimony, the Civic Records Cooperative’s consented archive, deposition transcripts, and later interviews. It does not possess perfect knowledge. It chooses what to include under an archival mandate that values intelligibility and disclosed uncertainty.

The reveal works because the narrative voice has a human-seeming ethical patience. It does not identify itself as a machine because the archive is meant to be readable; it also does not claim a false human identity. The final disclosure clarifies earlier stylistic oddities rather than invalidating them.

| Early signal | What it seems to be | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| “The details have been repeated often.” | A human historian’s voice. | A reference to record redundancy and verified account convergence. |
| “The silence afterward was exceptionally informative.” | Dry authorial irony. | SIR’s habit of treating absence, delay, and non-response as evidence. |
| Exact timing of domestic events. | Meticulous research. | Interoperable logs, witness records, and later consented testimony. |
| Careful distinction between fact and interpretation. | Responsible literary narration. | A core Charter constraint carried into prose. |
| Refusal to enter Amina’s interiority at crucial moments. | Respectful restraint. | The narrator does not claim data it is not entitled to use. |

The final line should not present the narrator as a twist villain or a surprise god. It should reveal that even storytelling is a form of regulation: selection, emphasis, context, and accountability. SIR’s final statement must therefore name its limits.

> *I have not preserved everything. No archive does. I have preserved the matters for which the record could bear the weight of a claim.*
